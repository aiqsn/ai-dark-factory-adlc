Definition

What is a dark factory?

A software delivery system in which AI agents do the repetitive work of turning specifications into tested, deployed software. People supply what agents cannot: the judgement about what is worth building, the definition of what good looks like, and the decisions on anything risky or unclear.

Purpose

Why build one?

So that delivery is limited by how clearly people can specify and judge work, not by how many engineer-hours they have. Human judgement stays at the centre; agents carry it out at scale.

What stays human

Good judgement still comes from people

  • What is worth building. People choose the work and write the spec with AI; agents can draft, not decide what matters.
  • What good looks like. Criteria, hidden scenarios, rubrics, probes and alert thresholds are written by people.
  • The hard calls. Every escalation, risky change and rollback that needs a decision goes to a person.
  • Accountability. A named person owns each standard the factory uses and answers for what it ships.

Setup

About an hour, in four tracks

  • Repo content and credentials can be done in any order.
  • Wire up secrets, labels and branch rules, then do a dry run with a trivial spec.
  • Deploys stay off until you set DEPLOY_ENABLED=true.

Least privilege

Each job holds only what it needs

  • Build job: model keys and the factory token, never the scenarios token.
  • Verify job: the read-only scenarios token, and no model keys.
  • Deploy job: production-environment secrets the other jobs can't read.

The workflow files

Three workflows, two handoffs

  • factory-build turns a labelled issue into a pull request.
  • factory-verify tests, reviews, then merges or escalates.
  • deploy ships each merge in stages, with probes at every step.

Adversarial reviews

Every reviewer is told to find a reason to reject

The spec is attacked before the build. Every PR faces reward-hacking probes, mutation testing and two hostile reviewers, and the hidden holdouts grow every week.

Deploying

A merge reaches users in stages

Upload with no traffic and probe the preview, send a 10% canary, then promote to 100%. Any failed probe returns traffic to the stable version in seconds and opens an alert.

Defence in depth

Starter kit

Caveats

Before you turn the lights off

  • Free models rotate monthly; expect to re-pick them every few weeks.
  • Free tiers may keep prompts, so use this for open-source or non-sensitive code.
  • Keep a person in the loop until the nightly metrics say otherwise.
  • Hidden holdouts are the best cheap answer to agents writing both code and tests.
